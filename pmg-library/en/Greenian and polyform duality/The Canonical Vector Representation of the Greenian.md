[[ru/Гриниан и дуальность полиформ/Каноническое векторное представление гриниана|Русский]]

Like the Laplacian, the Greenian has a canonical vector representation: both grade-one forms are sums of quadratic forms of vertex differences. Their exponentials are boundary polyforms. The coefficient for a pair is the negative inner product of the corresponding centroid vectors in the original resistance geometry. Thus the exponential is calculated using the same boundary merging and annihilation rules as the Laplacian exponential.

This is the main representation used in the series. Polar forms, cut forms, and point forms give other expressions for the same object. The vector expression is distinguished because its coefficients are unique for fixed vertices, and the products of its terms are described directly by forests. The coefficients may be negative, so the forest and spanning-tree sums are signed.

Examples of the complete graph, star, cycles, a weighted path, the graph from the article on the Kirchhoff matrix, and the Petersen graph are examined in [[Greenian Exponentials of Standard Graphs]].

## Setting and main result

Consider a connected undirected graph on $n\ge2$ vertices with positive conductances on its existing edges. Write differences as $a_{ij}=a_j-a_i=(a_i a_j)$; the space containing the elements will be specified separately. Let

$$
L=\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}[a_{ij}]^2,\qquad
M=\exp L,\qquad d=n-1
$$

In this formula, the vertices are direct and linearly independent. We denote the Greenian by $\mathrm{Gr}$ and its matrix by $\mathbf{Gr}=\mathbf L^+$. The Greenian exponential in the dual space is denoted by $D=\exp Q_1$ and is related to $M$ by its action on the top component $M_d$:

$$
M_d/Q_k=M_{d-k},\qquad D=\sum_{k=0}^{d}Q_k,\qquad Q_0=e
$$

The operation $/$ is linear exterior deletion, as established in [[Laplacian and Greenian - Mutual Duality of Exponentials]]. On the dual side, $a_i$ denote classes of coordinate functionals, and

$$
a_1+\cdots+a_n=0
$$

> [!info] Setting and main result
> **Theorem.** The canonical vector representation is
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> Q_1=\sum_{i<j}\gamma_{ij}[a_{ij}]^2,
> \qquad \gamma_{ij}=-\mathrm{Gr}_{ij},
> \qquad D=\exp Q_1
> }
> \tag{1}
> $$
>
> Here $a_{ij}$ are differences of dual elements, and $\mathbf{Gr}=\mathbf L^+$ is the Green matrix of the original graph. In transition formulas, write the dual elements explicitly as $a_i^\vee$. The centered identification $\mathcal C:a_i^\vee\mapsto p_i=a_i-h$ maps them to direct vectors, giving the same expression in the form
>
> $$
> \mathrm{Gr}=\sum_{i<j}\gamma_{ij}[a_{ij}]^2,\qquad
> \mathcal C(Q_1)=\mathrm{Gr},\qquad
> \mathcal C(D)=\exp(\mathrm{Gr})
> $$
>
> In this formula, $a_{ij}$ are now direct vectors: subtracting the centroid preserves differences. Thus $Q_1$ is the dual representation of the Greenian, not a separate type of form. The sum runs over all pairs, including pairs with no edge in the original graph. Zero coefficients may be omitted.
>
> Below we prove uniqueness of the vector expression, the coefficient formula, and the forest expansion of the exponential. The general law $Q_k=Q_1^k/k!$ follows from [[The Exterior Exponential and Representations of the Greenian#The dual exponential of a nondegenerate symmetric form|duality of the exterior exponential]]: in matched direct and dual bases, the first dual form has the inverse coefficient matrix.

## Why the vector representation is canonical

Let $E$ be generated by $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ with the sole relation $\sum_i a_i=0$. Then $\dim E=n-1$. We consider symmetric grade-one forms, that is, $\operatorname{Sym}^2(E)$.

> [!info] Why the vector representation is canonical
> **Theorem.** The forms $[a_{ij}]^2$, $i<j$, form a basis of $\operatorname{Sym}^2(E)$. In particular, every symmetric form $S$ has a unique expression
>
> $$
> S=\sum_{i<j}\gamma_{ij}[a_{ij}]^2
> $$

> [!note]- Proof
> **Proof.** Identify $E$ with the subspace of columns whose entries sum to zero by $a_i\mapsto\mathbf J e_i$, where $e_i$ are the standard coordinate columns and
>
> $$
> \mathbf J=\mathbf I-\frac1n\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}
> $$
>
> Every symmetric form $S$ corresponds to a unique symmetric matrix $\mathbf K$ with $\mathbf K\mathbf1=0$: its matrix on this subspace, extended by zero on constant columns. Therefore
>
> $$
> S=\sum_{i,j}K_{ij}[a_i,a_j]
> $$
>
> Expanding the squared differences and using $K_{ii}=-\sum_{j\ne i}K_{ij}$, we obtain
>
> $$
> S=-\sum_{i<j}K_{ij}[a_{ij}]^2
> $$
>
> Conversely, the matrix of the vector sum has off-diagonal entries $-\gamma_{ij}$ and zero row sums. Thus $S$ uniquely determines $\mathbf K$ and all $\gamma_{ij}$. $\square$

The representation is canonical relative to the given labeled vertex set. No reference vertex, tree, or cut decomposition is required. Relabeling the vertices permutes the coefficients together with their pairs.

Polar forms also form a basis, but in this series “the canonical working representation” means the vector representation. This selects the main notation; it does not assert that no other bases exist.

## Coefficients and centroid geometry

Let $h=n^{-1}\sum_i a_i$ be the centroid of the direct vertices. The [[The Exterior Exponential and Representations of the Greenian#The centered image and the Greenian|centered identification]]

$$
\mathcal C:a_i^\vee\longmapsto p_i=a_i-h
$$

maps the dual representation $Q_1$ to the Greenian on direct vectors. Here $a_i^\vee$ is a dual element and $a_i$ an independent direct vertex. In particular, $\mathcal C(a_j^\vee-a_i^\vee)=p_j-p_i=a_{ij}$; the map preserves exterior products. The coefficient matrix of the centered image of $Q_1$ is $\mathbf{Gr}$. The preceding theorem therefore gives $\gamma_{ij}=-\mathrm{Gr}_{ij}$, proving the coefficient statement in (1).

In the original resistance metric, the Green matrix is the Gram matrix of the centroid vectors:

$$
\boxed{
\gamma_{ij}=-\mathrm{Gr}_{ij}
=-(a_i-h)\cdot(a_j-h)
=-(a_i h)\cdot(a_j h)
}
\tag{2}
$$

The inner products in (2) involve direct vectors. Reversing both directions preserves their product. The inner product is taken in the metric $M$, not in a metric newly constructed from the Greenian exponential. As proved in [[The Greenian Metric and Potentials]], the normalized potentials for $N=\exp(\mathrm{Gr})$ are, conversely, given by the original Laplacian: $P_N([a_i-h,a_j-h])=L_{ij}$. That note also examines the potentials of vectors, cuts, and higher boundaries.

| Representation | Coefficient of a pair form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Direct Laplacian $L$ | $c_{ij}$ | Conductance of the original edge |
| Greenian $\mathrm{Gr}$; dual expression $Q_1$ | $\gamma_{ij}=-\mathrm{Gr}_{ij}$ | Negative inner product of centroid vectors |

An acute angle between centroid vectors gives $\gamma_{ij}<0$, an obtuse angle gives $\gamma_{ij}>0$, and orthogonality gives $\gamma_{ij}=0$. The presence of an original edge alone does not determine the sign.

The form $Q_1$ is positive definite on its $(n-1)$-dimensional space. This is compatible with negative coefficients in the chosen basis of quadratic forms of differences. The original graph conductances remain positive; $\gamma_{ij}$ are coefficients in a different representation.

## Mean resistances and the inverse distance transformation

Retain the following notation for resistance sums and means:

$$
R_i=\sum_jR_{ij},\qquad
\bar R_i=\frac{R_i}{n},\qquad
\bar R=\frac1{n^2}\sum_iR_i
$$

The means include the zero diagonal $R_{ii}=0$. Their denominators are $n$ and $n^2$, not $n-1$ and $n(n-1)$.

The [[Laplacian, Green Matrix, and Effective Resistance Matrix#Reconstructing the Green matrix|double-centering formula]]

$$
\mathbf{Gr}=-\frac12\mathbf J\mathbf R\mathbf J
$$

gives

$$
\boxed{
\gamma_{ij}
=\frac12\bigl(R_{ij}-\bar R_i-\bar R_j+\bar R\bigr),\qquad i\ne j
}
\tag{3}
$$

Thus the coefficient is half the deviation of the pair resistance from the baseline $\bar R_i+\bar R_j-\bar R$. In PMG research terminology, this transition was called the deviation transformation. The standard matrix term is double centering; recovering a centered Gram matrix from distances belongs to the classical method of principal coordinates [1]. Here the sign changes when passing from Gram matrix entries to coefficients of vector forms.

If $\mathscr L(\gamma)$ denotes the Laplacian matrix assembled from the pair coefficients $\gamma_{ij}$, then

$$
\mathscr L(\gamma)=\mathbf{Gr},\qquad
\mathcal D(\mathscr L(\gamma))=\mathbf R
$$

Here $\mathcal D(\mathbf A)_{ij}=A_{ii}+A_{jj}-2A_{ij}$. Thus formula (3) is the inverse transformation for the pair operator $\mathcal F=\mathcal D\circ\mathscr L$. For $n\ge3$, the [[The Distance Operator and KVP Decomposition#The inverse transformation by components|KVP decomposition of this operator]] gives

$$
\gamma=\frac1{2n}R_K+\frac1nR_V+\frac12R_P
$$

This is an equality of pair tables with zero diagonal. It does not mean applying the KVP projectors directly to the Green matrix, whose diagonal is nonzero.

## Multiplication of vector forms and the exponential

For a finite set of dual vertices $C=\{b_1,\ldots,b_m\}$, define the boundary notation through differences:

$$
(C)=(b_2-b_1)\cdots(b_m-b_1),\qquad (b_1)=1
$$

Permuting the vertices changes only the sign. This polynomial expression is the familiar simplex boundary transferred to dual arguments. It does not require an affine-weight functional on $E$ taking the value $1$ on every $a_i$: such a functional would be incompatible with $\sum_i a_i=0$.

The boundary product rules remain valid. For example,

$$
[(ab)]^2[(bc)]^2=[(abc)]^2,\qquad
[(ab)]^2[(ab)]^2=0,\qquad
[(ab)]^2[(bc)]^2[(ca)]^2=0
$$

Trees sharing vertices combine according to the merging rules; a cycle gives linearly dependent differences and makes the product vanish. For disjoint components, the product of their boundaries remains. The general rules are given in [[Basic Objects and Operations of Polyform Algebra#Products of simplex boundaries]].

For the form $f_{ij}=\gamma_{ij}[a_{ij}]^2$, we have $f_{ij}^2=0$, and the forms commute. Therefore

$$
\boxed{
D=\exp Q_1
=\prod_{i<j}\bigl(e+\gamma_{ij}[a_{ij}]^2\bigr)
}
\tag{4}
$$

The unit comes from choosing $e$ in every factor. Grade two selects two distinct pairs, grade three selects three. In $Q_1^k$, each set of $k$ distinct factors occurs $k!$ times; division by $k!$ counts it once.

We use the vector factors (4) to calculate the exponential. Any equal polar or cut expression gives the same exponential; it has no separate multiplication rule. The advantage of vector factors is that their nonzero products immediately have a forest interpretation.

## Signed forest grades

Regard all vertex pairs as edges of the complete graph with real weights $\gamma_{ij}$. This is an auxiliary weighted system; it is neither a planar dual graph nor necessarily a network of positive conductances.

For a forest $F$, set

$$
\gamma(F)=\prod_{\{i,j\}\in F}\gamma_{ij},\qquad
\Psi_F=[(C_1)\cdots(C_{n-k})]^2
$$

Here $k$ is the number of edges, and $C_1,\ldots,C_{n-k}$ are the forest components, including isolated vertices. The arguments of $\Psi_F$ are dual.

> [!info] Signed forest grades
> **Theorem.** All grades of the canonical vector exponential are given by
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> Q_k=\sum_{\substack{F\text{ a forest on }\mathcal V\\|E(F)|=k}}
> \gamma(F)\Psi_F
> }
> \tag{5}
> $$

> [!note]- Proof
> **Proof.** Expand product (4). A term of grade $k$ selects $k$ distinct edges. If they contain a cycle, the term vanishes. If the selected edges form a forest, the product of their differences equals the product of the component boundaries up to sign. The sign disappears in the quadratic form, and the coefficient is the product of the $\gamma_{ij}$.
>
> The relation $\sum_i a_i=0$ causes no additional vanishing for an individual forest. Before passing to dual classes, the coefficient columns of differences lie in $\mathbf1^\perp$, and the projection $\mathbf J$ is the identity on that subspace. Thus independence of the forest edges is preserved. $\square$

A negative contribution in (5) comes from a negative product of weights, not from the orientation of the exterior argument. Different forests may yield identical or linearly related forms; their contributions then add with their signs.

Two expansions of the same $Q_k$ must be distinguished:

| Expansion | Indexing forests | Weights | Dual arguments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vector expansion (5) | $k$ edges, $n-k$ components in the auxiliary system | Products of $\gamma_{ij}$, possibly signed | Component boundaries |
| Expansion over forests of the original graph | $n-1-k$ edges, $k+1$ components | $w(F)/\tau\ge0$ | Products of block sums |

The expansion in the second row is proved in [[Cuts and Forest Grades of the Greenian Exponential]]. It is the full forms that are equal, not individual forests or their weights. The first row is used for vector calculations; the second connects to the original combinatorics and vertex variations.

## The spanning-tree sum and normalization of the top form

On the dual side, set

$$
T^\vee=[a_1\cdots a_{n-1}]^2,\qquad
\Theta=[(a_1\ldots a_n)]^2
$$

These are different top forms: the first uses the exterior product of vertices, and the second the full boundary.

> [!info] The spanning-tree sum and normalization of the top form
> **Lemma.** Under $\sum_i a_i=0$,
>
> $$
> \boxed{\Theta=n^2T^\vee}
> \tag{6}
> $$

> [!note]- Proof
> **Proof.** Choose the basis $a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1}$ and substitute $a_n=-\sum_{i<n}a_i$. The differences $a_i-a_n$ have column matrix $\mathbf I_{n-1}+\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}$. Its eigenvalues are $n$ on the constant column and $1$ on its complement, so its determinant is $n$. The product of the differences equals the full boundary up to sign. Therefore $\Theta=n^2[a_1\cdots a_{n-1}]^2$. $\square$

Denote the signed spanning-tree sum of the vector coefficients by

$$
\tau_\gamma=\sum_{F\text{ a spanning tree on }\mathcal V}\gamma(F)
$$

> [!info] The spanning-tree sum and normalization of the top form
> **Theorem.** For the basic normalization $Q_0=e$,
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> \tau_\gamma=\frac1{n^2\tau},\qquad
> Q_d=\tau_\gamma\Theta=\frac1\tau T^\vee
> }
> \tag{7}
> $$

> [!note]- Proof
> **Proof.** By (5), all trees give the same form $\Theta$, so $Q_d=\tau_\gamma\Theta$. By the [[Laplacian and Greenian - Mutual Duality of Exponentials#Normalization and grade zero|definition of duality]], $Q_d=T^\vee/\tau$. Substituting (6) gives (7). $\square$

Thus the vector expansion involves both sums and differences of products of coefficients. The result is positive and equals $1/(n^2\tau)$. This is a weighted signed spanning-tree sum, not the number of trees in the original graph. The coefficient of the vertex top form $T^\vee$ is $1/\tau$; the factor $n^2$ cannot be dropped when changing the top argument.

### An independent matrix check

The Laplacian matrix of the weights $\gamma$ is $\mathbf{Gr}$. Kirchhoff's theorem is a polynomial identity and allows real weights of either sign; the more general all-minors theorem is presented in [2]. Therefore

$$
\tau_\gamma=\det\mathbf{Gr}_{\widehat r,\widehat r}
$$

If $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}$ are the nonzero eigenvalues of the original Laplacian, then

$$
\tau=\frac1n\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\lambda_j,\qquad
\tau_\gamma=\frac1n\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\lambda_j^{-1}
=\frac1{n^2\tau}
$$

This check recovers (7) from the cofactor and spectrum without using deletion by dual forms.

The centered identification preserves differences and maps $\Theta$ to the direct top form $T$. Therefore

$$
\mathcal C(T^\vee)=\frac1{n^2}T,\qquad
\mathcal C(Q_d)=\frac1{n^2\tau}T
$$

The last form is the top grade of the Greenian exponential. This reconciles its spanning coefficient with the basic normalization of duality.

## Full example: a path on four vertices

Let the original path $a-b-c-d$ have unit conductances. Then $\tau=1$, and resistances equal distances along the path:

$$
(R_a,R_b,R_c,R_d)=(6,4,4,6),\qquad
(\bar R_a,\bar R_b,\bar R_c,\bar R_d)
=\left(\frac32,1,1,\frac32\right),\qquad
\bar R=\frac54
$$

Formula (3) gives

| Pair | $R_{ij}$ | $\gamma_{ij}$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| $ab$ | $1$ | $-1/8$ |
| $ac$ | $2$ | $3/8$ |
| $ad$ | $3$ | $5/8$ |
| $bc$ | $1$ | $1/8$ |
| $bd$ | $2$ | $3/8$ |
| $cd$ | $1$ | $-1/8$ |

Consequently,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_1=\frac18\bigl(&-[(ab)]^2+3[(ac)]^2+5[(ad)]^2+{}\\
&+[(bc)]^2+3[(bd)]^2-[(cd)]^2\bigr)
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

### Grade two from vector factors

First combine edge pairs sharing a vertex. For the triple $abc$, for example, the total coefficient of $[(abc)]^2$ is

$$
\gamma_{ab}\gamma_{ac}+
\gamma_{ab}\gamma_{bc}+
\gamma_{ac}\gamma_{bc}
=\frac{-3-1+3}{64}=-\frac1{64}
$$

The three pairs of disjoint edges retain products of boundaries. As a result,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_2=\frac1{64}\bigl(&-[(abc)]^2+7[(abd)]^2+7[(acd)]^2-[(bcd)]^2+{}\\
&+[(ab)(cd)]^2+9[(ac)(bd)]^2+5[(ad)(bc)]^2\bigr)
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$

The coefficients are obtained directly from products of vector factors. Substituting $d=-a-b-c$ and expanding the exterior arguments reduces (9) to

$$
Q_2=[ab]^2+[ad]^2+[cd]^2
$$

Parentheses matter: $[(ab)]^2$ has grade $1$, while $[ab]^2$ has grade $2$.

### Grade three and cancellation of spanning-tree contributions

Each of the 16 trees on four vertices gives $\Theta=[(abcd)]^2$. The products of weights have common denominator $8^3=512$. Grouping by the number of negative edges gives

| Negative edges in the tree | Number of trees | Total contribution to $\tau_\gamma$ |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $0$ | $4$ | $84/512$ |
| $1$ | $8$ | $-64/512$ |
| $2$ | $4$ | $12/512$ |
| Total | $16$ | $32/512=1/16$ |

Consequently,

$$
Q_3=\frac1{16}[(abcd)]^2=[abc]^2
$$

The last equality uses $[(abcd)]^2=16[abc]^2$, not a change in the exponential coefficients. Thus

$$
D=e+Q_1+Q_2+Q_3
$$

The equivalent short cut expression

$$
Q_1=[a]^2+[a+b]^2+[d]^2
$$

provides an independent check: its powers give the same $Q_2,Q_3$. The vector calculation shows how ordinary boundary merging and cancellation of signed weights produce the result.

## Checks on an edge and a complete graph

For a single edge of conductance $c>0$, with $a+b=0$,

$$
\gamma_{ab}=\frac1{4c},\qquad
Q_1=\frac1{4c}[(ab)]^2=\frac1c[a]^2
$$

This checks the factor $n^2=4$ in the smallest dimension.

For the complete graph $K_n$ with uniform conductance $c>0$,

$$
\mathbf L=nc\mathbf J,\qquad
\mathbf{Gr}=\frac1{nc}\mathbf J,\qquad
\gamma_{ij}=\frac1{n^2c}\quad(i\ne j)
$$

All coefficients are positive here. By Cayley's formula,

$$
\tau=n^{n-2}c^{n-1},\qquad
\tau_\gamma=n^{n-2}\left(\frac1{n^2c}\right)^{n-1}
=\frac1{n^2\tau}
$$

Thus negative weights are possible and significant, but need not occur for every graph.

## Other representations of the same form

Polarization recovers the expression

$$
Q_1=-\frac12\sum_{i<j}R_{ij}\{a_i,a_j\},\qquad
\{a_i,a_j\}=[a_i,a_j]+[a_j,a_i]
$$

It is convenient for extracting resistances and [[Greenian Coefficients and Vertex Variations|interpreting variations]]. The mixed point and vector expression is

$$
Q_1=\frac12\sum_{i<j}R_{ij}[a_{ij}]^2
-\frac12\sum_iR_i[a_i]^2
$$

Its point and vector forms are linearly related:

$$
\sum_j[a_{ij}]^2=n[a_i]^2+\sum_j[a_j]^2
$$

Thus the mixed expansion requires the stated coefficient rule. Splitting it into two parts is not a decomposition into independent subspaces.

[[Cuts and Forest Grades of the Greenian Exponential]] gives positive coefficients through forests of the original graph. These coefficients belong to the chosen cut representation; unlike coefficients in the vector basis, they may change when an equal form is rewritten.

## Status of results and working order

The canonical nature of the vector expression, the coefficient formulas, the forest expansion, and the relation between the two top normalizations are proved above. The exponential law and centered identification rely on the cited proofs in [[The Exterior Exponential and Representations of the Greenian]]. All graph statements in this note concern the symmetric case. The balanced extension requires bilinear forms and separate attention to argument order.

Nine graphs on 2–6 vertices were checked in exact rational arithmetic: a weighted edge, paths, a weighted path, a cycle, complete graphs, and a graph with nonuniform weights. For each graph, four calculations of every grade were compared: expansion of vector factors, enumeration of signed forests, minors of the inverse reduced Laplacian, and positive forest sums of the original graph. Spanning-tree sums were checked independently against Green matrix cofactors. Both deletion directions were also checked for every grade: $M_d/Q_k=M_{d-k}$ and $Q_d/M_k=Q_{d-k}$. The [verification code](https://github.com/magin67/pmg-notes/blob/v5/checks/dual/verify_vector.py) uses only the Python standard library; run `python checks/dual/verify_vector.py`.

To work with the Greenian exponential, first determine $\gamma_{ij}$ and construct the vector factors. Use the polar representation to read off resistances, the cut representation for connection with forests of the original graph, and the centered representation for the Green matrix. These transitions preserve the form and its entire exponential.
