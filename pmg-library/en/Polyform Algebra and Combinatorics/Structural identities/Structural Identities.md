This series determines which partition forms are independent, how to express the remaining forms in terms of them, and how many independent identities relate the original forms. The motivating application is gluing graphs along several vertices: forests in each piece induce partitions of the shared vertices, and structural identities reduce the number of coordinates needed to compute the spanning-tree coefficient.

The starting example is the **tetrahedron identity**. It is an equality of forms before a metric is chosen. Relations between areas follow from metric evaluation and are equalities of numbers. In PMG, the norm is quadratic: $X^2=X\cdot X$; the form $[X]^2$ must be distinguished from its numerical evaluation.

## Prerequisites

The algebraic background is developed in [[Basic Objects and Operations of Polyform Algebra]]: boundaries, grades, forms, and their product. [[Metric of Higher-Grade Objects]] is useful for the geometric example, and [[Laplacian Exponential]] for the graph application.

Notation follows the [[Core specification|Core Specification]] and [[PMG terminology|Terminology]]. A block and a component of a partition mean the same thing. The size of a block is its number of vertices; the grade of the corresponding boundary is one less.

## Reading order

1. **[[Partition Forms and the Tetrahedron Identity]].** The definition of a partition form, a proof of the first identity, its geometric interpretation, and a numerical example. This already shows why equal polyforms may have different coefficient sums.

2. **[[Constructing Structural Identities]].** Block sizes and component grades, transfer and multiplication of identities, and the general four-block pattern. Examples on five and six vertices and the passage to symmetric sums.

3. **[[Basis and Dimension of the Space of Partition Forms]].** Proof that the noncrossing forms span the space and are independent, Narayana and Catalan numbers, the relation count, and completeness of the transformations.

4. **[[Structural Identities and Graph Gluing]].** Grove polyforms on shared vertices, the gluing formula, and coordinate transformations. Examples include a weighted cycle and gluing two paths into $K_4$.

5. **[[Supports of Structural Identities]].** The distinction between the support of a partition and that of a relation, transfer from a smaller set, decomposition of the relation space, and the precise meaning of new independent directions.

**Main route:** follow the definitions, figures, and worked examples in the listed order. In the basis note, start with “seven forms — six coordinates”; in the support note, compare transfer with multiplication and follow the count $30+10=40$.

**Further study:** return to the general basis proof, the pairing matrix, generating series, and the decomposition of the relation space. These sections explain why the patterns seen in the examples hold in general.

## Main results



### A noncrossing basis and the exact dimension

For a fixed cyclic order, noncrossing partition forms constitute a basis. On four vertices in grade $2$, this means **six forms instead of seven**; across all grades, $14$ instead of $15$. The general dimensions are Narayana and Catalan numbers. See the [[Basis and Dimension of the Space of Partition Forms|basis note]] for the example, formulas, and proof.

### Completeness of the four-block identity

All structural relations are generated by the tetrahedron identity expanded to four nonempty disjoint blocks, with additional unchanged blocks allowed. [[Constructing Structural Identities]] explains the operations through examples; the [[Basis and Dimension of the Space of Partition Forms#Completeness of the four-block identity|completeness proof]] establishes the general result.

### The spanning-tree coefficient of a glued graph

Grove polyforms on the vertices shared by two pieces determine the spanning-tree coefficient of their union. The noncrossing basis reduces the number of coordinates while preserving the pairing result. The [[Structural Identities and Graph Gluing|gluing note]] begins with two paths forming a cycle, then works through the coordinate transformation for $K_4$.

### Relations with small supports

Transfer by adjoining singleton blocks preserves grade and support. In grade $r\geqslant1$, all relations are generated by transfers from sets of at most $2r$ vertices; a sum may have a larger support. The [[Supports of Structural Identities|support note]] illustrates this distinction and explains how new independent directions appear.

## Status and scope of the series

The general algebraic claims concern forms on independent basis vertices over $\mathbb R$, before a graph or metric is chosen. Substituting points in a lower-dimensional space may introduce additional dependencies. The graph application concerns finite connected undirected loopless graphs with positive conductances on existing edges; planarity is not assumed.

Proofs of the tetrahedron identity, its extension, and the gluing formula are given directly. The general basis proof uses the Kenyon-Wilson algorithm and nonsingularity of the noncrossing partition pairing matrix, which follows from the meander determinant; precise references and the passage to PMG forms are given in the [[Basis and Dimension of the Space of Partition Forms]]. The support result relies on this basis. Computational checks verify examples and do not replace general proofs.

Symmetric polyforms retain their own series: only the connection with symmetric sums is shown here. Reductions, toponomes, the spanning-tree operator, and the dual polyform are not needed for the proved results. Choosing a graph decomposition remains a separate problem. Full forest pairing is treated in the [[The Four-Point Passport and State Reduction|boundary-polynomial series]]; it cannot automatically be replaced by this series’ spanning-tree pairing.
