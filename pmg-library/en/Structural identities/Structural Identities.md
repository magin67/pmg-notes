This series determines which partition forms are independent, how to express the remaining forms in terms of them, and how many independent identities relate the original forms. The motivating application is gluing graphs along several vertices: forests in each piece induce partitions of the shared vertices, and structural identities reduce the number of coordinates needed to compute the spanning-tree coefficient.

The starting example is the **tetrahedron identity**. It is an equality of forms before a metric is chosen. Relations between areas follow from metric evaluation and are equalities of numbers. In PMG, the norm is quadratic: $X^2=X\cdot X$; the form $[X]^2$ must be distinguished from its numerical evaluation.

## Main results

### A noncrossing basis and the exact dimension

**The main algebraic result:** for a fixed cyclic order, the forms of noncrossing partitions constitute a basis of the space of all partition forms. Every partition form has a unique expansion in this basis, with integer coefficients.

For $n\geqslant1$ independent basis vertices, let $\mathcal P_{n,r}$ be the space of partition forms of grade $r$, and let $\mathcal P_n$ be the direct sum over all grades. Grade $r$ corresponds to partitions into $n-r$ blocks. For $0\leqslant r\leqslant n-1$,

$$
\dim\mathcal P_{n,r}
=N(n,n-r)
=\frac1n\binom nr\binom n{r+1},
\qquad
\dim\mathcal P_n=C_n=\frac1{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}
$$

Here $N(n,k)$ is a Narayana number and $C_n$ is a Catalan number. The cyclic order selects a basis but does not change the dimension; graph planarity is not required. The proof and a four-vertex example are given in the [[Basis and Dimension of the Space of Partition Forms#Noncrossing partitions and the basis|third note]].

### Completeness of the four-block identity

**All structural relations among partition forms are generated by one pattern:** the tetrahedron identity extended to four nonempty pairwise disjoint blocks, with additional unchanged blocks allowed. Every relation is a linear combination of instances of this pattern.

This establishes completeness of the transformations, beyond providing a way to construct individual examples. The number of independent relations in grade $r$ is $I_{n,r}=S(n,n-r)-N(n,n-r)$, where $S(n,k)$ is a Stirling number of the second kind. See the [[Constructing Structural Identities#The tetrahedron identity for four blocks|four-block identity]] and the [[Basis and Dimension of the Space of Partition Forms#Completeness of the four-block identity|proof of completeness]].

### The spanning-tree coefficient of a glued graph

**The main application:** the spanning-tree coefficient of a glued graph can be computed from polyforms defined only on the vertices shared by the two pieces.

Let the connected pieces $G_1,G_2$ share a set $S$ of $k\geqslant1$ vertices and have disjoint edge sets. The polyform $P_i$ collects partition forms on $S$, with coefficients equal to sums of grove weights in $G_i$. A grove is a spanning forest in which every component contains at least one vertex of $S$. Then

$$
\tau(G_1\cup G_2)=\tau_S(P_1P_2)
$$

The functional $\tau_S$ extracts the coefficient of the spanning-tree form on $S$. Passing to the noncrossing basis leaves $C_k$ coordinates for each polyform and preserves all such pairings. For example, with four shared vertices, $14$ forms suffice in place of $15$, and in grade $2$, $6$ suffice in place of $7$.

The [[Structural Identities and Graph Gluing#The spanning-tree coefficient after gluing|gluing formula]] is proved by splitting a spanning tree into two groves. It also permits computation from a prescribed graph decomposition. This coordinate reduction does not automatically extend to the full unrooted spanning-forest polynomial.

### Relations with small supports

**The relation count is decomposed by the number of participating vertices**, with a corresponding result proved for the relation space itself.

Here transfer means adjoining singleton blocks. The support of a partition consists of the vertices in its blocks of size at least two; the support of a relation is the union of the supports of all its terms with nonzero coefficients. The relation basis is compatible with transfer. This makes it possible to count the new independent directions that appear as the allowed support size increases.

For fixed grade $r\geqslant1$, all relations are generated by transfers from sets of at most $2r$ vertices. A linear combination of such relations may have larger support. Being new relative to transfer does not rule out obtaining a relation by multiplying another identity. Proofs, counting coefficients, and examples are collected in the [[Supports of Structural Identities#Decomposing the relation space|fifth note]].

## Reading order

1. **[[Partition Forms and the Tetrahedron Identity]].** The definition of a partition form, a proof of the first identity, its geometric interpretation, and a numerical example. This already shows why equal polyforms may have different coefficient sums.

2. **[[Constructing Structural Identities]].** Block sizes and component grades, transfer and multiplication of identities, and the general four-block pattern. Examples on five and six vertices and the passage to symmetric sums.

3. **[[Basis and Dimension of the Space of Partition Forms]].** Proof that the noncrossing forms span the space and are independent, Narayana and Catalan numbers, the relation count, and completeness of the transformations.

4. **[[Structural Identities and Graph Gluing]].** Grove polyforms on shared vertices, the gluing formula, and coordinate transformations. Examples include a weighted cycle and gluing two paths into $K_4$.

5. **[[Supports of Structural Identities]].** The distinction between the support of a partition and that of a relation, transfer from a smaller set, decomposition of the relation space, and the precise meaning of new independent directions.

For a first reading, follow this order. To locate the main rank result quickly, use the third note; for the application to gluing, use the third and fourth; for new relations as vertices are added, use the third and fifth. These notes link to the definitions and proofs needed from the first two.

## Prerequisites

The algebraic background is developed in [[Basic Objects and Operations of Polyform Algebra]]: boundaries, grades, forms, and their product. [[Metric of Higher-Grade Objects]] is useful for the geometric example, and [[Laplacian Exponential]] for the graph application.

Notation follows the [[Core specification|core specification]] and [[PMG terminology|PMG terminology]]. A block and a component of a partition mean the same thing. The size of a block is its number of vertices; the grade of the corresponding boundary is one less.

## Status and scope of the series

The general algebraic claims concern forms on independent basis vertices over $\mathbb R$, before a graph or metric is chosen. Substituting points in a lower-dimensional space may introduce additional dependencies. The graph application concerns finite connected undirected loopless graphs with positive conductances on existing edges; planarity is not assumed.

Proofs of the tetrahedron identity, its extension, and the gluing formula are given directly. The general basis proof uses the Kenyon-Wilson algorithm and nonsingularity of the noncrossing partition pairing matrix, which follows from the meander determinant; precise references and the passage to PMG forms are given in the third note. The support result relies on this basis. Computational checks verify examples and do not replace general proofs.

Symmetric polyforms retain their own series: only the connection with symmetric sums is shown here. Reductions, toponomes, the spanning-tree operator, and the dual polyform are not needed for the proved results. Choosing a graph decomposition and the full forest pairing remain separate problems.
