What changes when couplings become directed and constructions become nonsymmetric?

## Series in this section

- **[[en/Directed Graphs/Balanced graphs/Balanced Graphs|Balanced Graphs]]** (12 notes). Balance, the bilinear Laplacian, circulation, one-way resistances, balancing, and directed forests.
- **[[ru/Направленные графы/Полиформы орграфов/Полиформы орграфов|Digraph Polyforms (Russian)]]**. A weighted, unbalanced digraph illustrates the forest expansion and how to extract the spanning-tree potential of a chosen root. Note 3 compares the arc form with the one-way resistance form on a directed three-cycle. Note 4 normalizes by the sum of the spanning-tree potentials and proves additivity for opposite directions. Notes 5 and 6 use the one-way resistance matrix in two ways. Together with the relative spanning-tree potentials it determines the arc weights. On its own it gives the directional pairing of two vectors. Notes 7 and 8 express the norm of a triangle boundary, and then of a simplex boundary of any grade, both as a Gram determinant and as a sum over starting vertices.

## Reading route

The prerequisite is [[en/Getting Started and Foundations/Introduction/PMG Introductory Series|Introductory Series]], especially the algebra of forms and the Laplacian exponential.

1. Start with balance and the bilinear Laplacian in [[en/Directed Graphs/Balanced graphs/Balanced Graphs|Balanced Graphs]], then proceed to the exponential and circulation.
2. For directional geometry, first use [[en/Resistance Geometry and Duality/Spaces/Spaces|Spaces]], then read about resistances, coordinates, and the sphere in the balanced series.
3. For directed data, proceed to the oriented extensions in [[en/Invariants, Decompositions, and Spectra/KVP decomposition/KVP Decomposition and Oriented Extensions|KVP Decomposition and Oriented Extensions]]; for forest toponomes, use [[en/Polyform Algebra and Combinatorics/Partition toponomes/Partition Toponomes|Partition Toponomes]] and [[en/Polyform Algebra and Combinatorics/Polynomial toponomes/Polynomial Toponomes|Polynomial Toponomes]].

Balance does not imply symmetry. Results from the symmetric spectral series and positive resistance geometry require separately checked conditions before being extended.
